# Outcome and computational handoff

## Strongest justified conclusion

The universal assertion is false: `(n,m)=(800,5)` is an admissible counterexample. After removing the forced factor `x^160`, the monic degree-640 polynomial has a negative order-20 power-sum Hankel determinant. The elementary Gram-matrix argument in `research/argument.md` proves that this forces a nonreal zero. Multiplicities cause no difficulty. No smallest-counterexample or other-cap classification is claimed.

## Computations actually completed

All arithmetic tests below were exact integers or rational numbers, except a displayed diagnostic ratio in the initial Newton sweep. That ratio was not used to decide any inequality.

* Initial box: all 1,532 pairs `5 <= m <= 12`, `m < n <= 200` passed all Newton inequalities after stripping the zero factor. Earlier rows were generated by the positive counting recurrence. The first invocation using the executable name `python` failed before any computation; the successful run used `python3`.
* Exact Sturm sub-box: all 572 pairs `5 <= m <= 12`, `m < n <= 80` were checked. Primitive integer negative remainders gave gcd degree zero and exactly `n-ceil(n/m)` distinct real zeros in every case. The run completed in about 19 seconds, within its 120-second budget. No cases of that sub-box were omitted. No full Sturm test was done for larger n.
* All 1,532 pairs in the original box passed positivity of the power sums of the negated roots through order 60. These are necessary conditions, not a proof of real-rootedness.
* Exploratory formal large-n moment sequence: for each `m=5,...,12`, computed `[t^k](g'_m(-t))^(-k)/(k+1)` through `k=100`; none was negative. For the sequence through order 40, tested unshifted and shifted leading Hankel minors up to order 20. At m=5 the first negative minors had orders 15 and 14, respectively. No nonpositive minors were found through order 20 for m=6,...,12. The asymptotic interpretation was only a search heuristic, and is not a dependency of the final certificate.
* Finite-n Hankel search used m=5, power sums through order 40, and maximum matrix order 20. Both unshifted and shifted tests passed at n=100,150,200,300,400. At n=800 the unshifted order-20 determinant was negative, and the search stopped. Planned n=1600,3200,6400,10000 were not tested. This discovery used all 800 roots, including zeros. The final certificate instead strips all 160 zero roots and independently confirms a negative order-20 determinant with `s_0=640`. The shifted order-20 determinant at n=800 was also checked and was negative; it is not needed.
* Final certification: constructed complete coefficient rows through n=800 at m=5, checking the differential recurrence at each row; independently recomputed the final entire coefficient vector by the generating-function identity. The two exact determinant algorithms agreed. Ran the standalone verifier twice successfully, first creating and then checking `certificates/witness.json`.

The finite searches were stopped on completion of their stated ranges, or on finding the witness; no timeout truncated them. Caps outside 5,...,12 were not computationally searched. No approximate zeros were computed. Passing the preliminary tests was inconclusive; the stronger moment-matrix condition supplied the obstruction.

## Reproduction and dependencies

`research/argument.md` gives the complete argument and a compact exact coefficient formula.
`code/check.py` generates and verifies the counterexample from scratch using Python 3's standard library only. Run `python3 code/check.py` from the artifact root. Python 3.14.4 was used here.
`certificates/witness.json` records the 39 leading coefficients and moments needed for the determinant, its exact value, and its leading-minor signs.

Only the final verifier and the Gram-matrix argument are needed to reproduce the refutation. Neither any limiting-moment assertion nor a numerical root-finding routine is a premise.
